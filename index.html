Growth Spot 1transact
First try'If the bank chooses to transact its business overseas, then it might get popular.' — close, but doesn't prove what transact means yet
✅Full corrected sentenceIf the bank chooses to transact its business overseas, then it will conduct or carry on business in another country.
💡 transact = conduct business
Growth Spot 2suffice
First try'A cup of sugar did not suffice for the recipe, but at least we won't get cavities.' — true, but doesn't yet explain why one cup wasn't enough
✅Full corrected sentenceA cup of sugar did not suffice for the recipe, but we needed more sugar because one cup was not enough.
💡 suffice = be enough
Growth Spot 3deficient
First try'Billy does not eat enough vegetables, so he is deficient in healthiness.' — good instinct, but deficient works best with a specific thing that's missing
✅Full corrected sentenceBilly does not eat enough vegetables, so he is deficient in vitamins or nutrients.
💡 deficient = lacking / not enough
Growth Spot 4facsimile
First try'We knew that the document was a facsimile, rather than being special.' — close, but doesn't yet contrast the facsimile with the real thing
✅Full corrected sentenceWe knew that the document was a facsimile, rather than the original contract.
💡 facsimile = a copy or imitation (note: the source sentence originally repeated "of the original contract" twice — tightened here so it reads cleanly)
Growth Spot 5agenda
First try'If the half-hour meeting lasts two hours, it probably means the agenda is short.' — almost backwards! a longer meeting usually means a longer agenda
✅Full corrected sentenceIf the half-hour meeting lasts two hours, it probably means the agenda is too long or has too many items.
💡 agenda = a list of things to discuss
Growth Spot 6composition
First try'Geologists studied the composition of the mysterious rock because it looked cool.' — fun reason, but doesn't connect to what composition means yet
✅Full corrected sentenceGeologists studied the composition of the mysterious rock because they wanted to know what it was made of.
💡 composition = what something is made of
Growth Spot 7deposit
First try'We wanted to deposit all the Halloween candy — we wanted a snack.' — good try, but doesn't say where the candy ended up
✅Full corrected sentenceWe wanted to deposit all the Halloween candy in a container, cupboard, or safe place.
💡 deposit = put / place / leave
Growth Spot 8impermanent
First try'We learned how impermanent sand castles could be when we went to the beach.' — true, but doesn't yet show what actually made them impermanent
✅Full corrected sentenceWe learned how impermanent sand castles could be when the waves washed them away.
💡 impermanent = temporary / not lasting
Growth Spot 9stationary
❌First tryThe storm system is currently constant and will probably not move for several more days.
✅Full corrected sentenceThe storm system is currently stationary and will probably not move for several more days.
💡 stationary = not moving · easy to mix up with constant (below) — stationary is about position, constant is about change over time
Growth Spot 10constant
❌First tryThe economy seems to change all the time; it is difficult to find any kind of stationary in it.
✅Full corrected sentenceThe economy seems to change all the time; it is difficult to find any kind of constant in it.
💡 constant = staying the same · this is the swap-back of Mistake 9 — same two words, opposite mix-up
Growth Spot 11composition
❌First tryAlthough various kinds of pollution are dumped into the ocean each year, its circumstance remains basically the same.
✅Full corrected sentenceAlthough various kinds of pollution are dumped into the ocean each year, its composition remains basically the same.
💡 composition = makeup / what it is made of · easy to mix up with circumstance (below) — composition is what it's made of, circumstance is the situation around it
Growth Spot 12circumstance
❌First tryWhen we thought about the composition that had caused our financial problems, we realized what we should have done differently.
✅Full corrected sentenceWhen we thought about the circumstance that had caused our financial problems, we realized what we should have done differently.
💡 circumstance = situation or condition around an event · this is the swap-back of Mistake 11 — same two words, opposite mix-up

FAC, FIC, FECT

Latin FACERE, FACTUM, “to make; to do”
FAC, FIC, FECT come from the Latin root meaning “make” or “do.”
FACSIMILE
n. a copy; an imitation
The edition of the US Constitution was a poor facsimile that looked like it was made on a cheap copier.
Syn: duplicate
DEFICIENT
adj. not having enough; lacking
The doctor said the patient was deficient in vitamins.
Syn: incomplete
PERFECT
v. to make as good as possible; to improve
The actor practiced the scene again and again to perfect it.
Syn: improve
AFFECTION
n. a feeling of love or liking; a tender feeling
Ladi bought his aunt a lovely present to show his affection.
Syn: fondness
SUFFICE
v. to be enough
Berry did not need any more friends; she felt that the ones she had would suffice.
Syn: satisfy
UNIT ONEPage 7

ACT, AG · OPER · FUNCT

More roots about doing, working, and performing
ACT/AG relate to doing; OPER and FUNCT relate to working or performing.
TRANSACT
v. to carry on or conduct
Since the office was closed, Carrie couldn't transact any business there today.
Syn: conduct
AGENDA
n. a list of things to do
The agenda included a report on income and one on spending.
Syn: plan
COAGULATE
v. to become thick, solid, clumped, or clotted
Chemicals in the body help blood coagulate.
Syn: thicken
OPERABLE
adj. in working order; able to work
The old mower was repaired until it became operable again.
Syn: usable
COOPERATE
v. to work together; be helpful
The football team had to cooperate with one another.
Syn: assist
OPERATIONAL
adj. in working order
The computer system was operational again after the repair.
Syn: working
FUNCTION
v. to work; to perform
One elevator did not function properly.
Syn: work
MALFUNCTION
n./v. a problem in the way something works
A computer malfunction made the screen stop.
Syn: failure

UNIT TWOPage 12

POS, PON · MAN

Latin roots about putting, placing, and remaining
POS/PON often carry the idea of putting or placing. MAN can carry the idea of staying or remaining.
DEPOSIT
v. to put down, drop, or place
The truck deposited its load beside the road.
Syn: deliver
COMPOSITION
n. the makeup of something
Bronze has a composition of copper and tin.
Syn: content
PROPOSITION
n. an idea put forth; a suggestion
A proposition was made to rebuild the nation's election system.
Syn: offer
OPPONENT
n. one who goes against; a rival
His opponent in the marathon race fell farther behind him.
Syn: competitor
IMPERMANENT
adj. not lasting forever
Few relationships last forever; most are impermanent.
Syn: passing
REMNANT
n. something left over
The only remnant of the building that could be found after the fire was a badly burned door.
Syn: remainder
UNIT TWOPage 13

STA, STAN

Latin STARE, STATUM, “to stand; to stay”
STA/STAN give the idea of standing or staying.
STATUS
n. a condition or situation; a position
The status of the patient remained stable.
Syn: position
CIRCUMSTANCE
n. the condition surrounding something; a situation
The police wondered what circumstance would make someone break into the building.
Syn: incident
CONSTANT
n./adj. something that stays the same; steady
Vince felt that the only constant in his life was change.
Syn: stable
STATIONARY
adj. standing still; not moving
Her bicycle was stationary at the light.
Syn: motionless

Reading comprehension using Unit Two vocabulary

For much of United States history, the country did not always have one permanent central banking system. Early national banks were created, challenged, and later allowed to expire. The Federal Reserve System was eventually established in the twentieth century.

A central bank can influence the money supply, interest rates, and the stability of a nation's financial system. Its status and responsibilities have changed over time as laws and economic circumstances changed.

Because banking policy affects businesses, consumers, and the government, debates about central banking have often involved competing propositions and strong opponents.
